I led a team that developed the Household Readiness Model for Pets (HRM-P), a quantitative system designed to evaluate household readiness for responsible pet ownership using advanced mathematical modeling, probabilistic simulations, and vector projection analysis. The model quantified both human well-being (HWB) and pet well-being (PWB) to balance companionship dynamics, employing a modified cosine similarity function and high-dimensional vector projections to assess household–pet compatibility across multiple species. I engineered Monte Carlo simulations that incorporated demographic and financial data to estimate state-by-state readiness and retention rates, while also extending the model to forecast how pet ownership trends might shift under different economic conditions. The HRM-P system adapted to various pet species and breeds by integrating statistical distributions, nonlinear analysis, and behavioral modeling to produce actionable insights for shelters and policymakers. This project showcased mathematical creativity and technical rigor in applying geometric and probabilistic principles to improve the sustainability of pet adoption and reduce abandonment.
